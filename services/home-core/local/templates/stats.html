The total

Against the budget

What each trailing window has cost. OpenCode Zen and together.ai are both billed per token and neither publishes an allowance, so the bar is the household's own budget — set it in assistant.spend_budget. Without one this is spend and nothing else. Either way it is an estimate from the token counts, not a bill.

Where it goes

Uncached input, cached input and output. About three fifths of the input arrives cached, so the cache price carries real weight — though not as much as the uncached one.

By turn type

Where each turn comes from: a phone notification, a chore reminder, a profession, or an ordinary conversation.

By routing

Which tier each turn started on and who decided — the caller, a sticky session, the classifier, or an escalation after a cheap attempt failed. Escalations from action are the classifier being wrong; a big complex share with none is it being cautious.

By model

By tool

What Alfred did, not just what it cost. Calls is counted per call — forty commands in one turn is exactly the shape worth seeing, the one that took four and a half minutes to answer how much a bill was. Everything else is counted per turn, because the tokens are spent by the turn and not by the tool: a turn that used two appears in both, which is why the percentages add up to more than 100%. Splitting the cost evenly would look tidier and would be invented.

By person

The house's own models

Vision, speech and listening, on this household's own card. None of it appears in any figure above, because none of it is billed — which is exactly why it went unmeasured. What it spends is the GPU, and on one card that is the scarcer of the two: a vision model and a voice competing for the same 12 GB is a decision, and a dollar column would say nothing about it.

Latency is the median and the 95th percentile of real calls, never a mean: an engine that is warm for an hour and then reloads has a mean nobody ever waited. Failed calls are counted and excluded from the timings, so a model does not look faster the more often it breaks.

Which path used which model

The same model can serve two jobs with different needs. The camera wall reviews every clip unattended and can take its time; a room waiting for an answer cannot. This is the table to read before moving either of them.

The card

What was in it

Peak is what a model needs — the number that decides whether it fits beside something else. Resident is how much of the window it was on the card at all, which is what separates a model worth keeping warm from one paged in for a clip and gone again. Only Ollama can name what it is holding, and it counts weights rather than the CUDA context, the vision encoder or the KV cache — so “other” is that undercount plus audio.cpp and the camera detector, neither of which reports a per-model size. It is shown rather than shared out, because splitting it would put a number on this table that nothing measured.
