# The Studio: how to use it

The Studio makes video, pictures, songs and voices on the house's own graphics
card, and records tutorials from your screen. Everything stays in the house.
Open it from the portal (Apps → Studio) or ask Alfred in the chat. **💬 Help** at
the top of the Studio answers questions about using it.

## Projects

- **New project** (＋ on the projects page): give it a name and pick what it is
  for. *Music video*, *Recording* (tutorials, screen and camera) and *Free* are
  ready; *short film*, *explainer* and *podcast* are coming.
- Anything you ask Alfred for without naming a project lands in your default
  project, called "Alfred".
- A project has tabs: **Video** (the shots or recordings, in order),
  **Storyboard** (a picture per shot, before any video), **Characters**,
  **Music & voices**, **Pictures** and **Files** (your uploads).
- **🕘 History** lists every change to the project's words (descriptions,
  lyrics, looks); any change can be undone alone, or the whole project taken
  back to how it was then.
- Every item keeps all its versions. A new version never deletes the old one.
  ⭐ marks the version to use. 🗑 on a version deletes that version's file. ✕ on
  a card takes the item out of the project (its files stay until the project is
  deleted).

## The card bar and the queue

- The bar at the top says what the card is doing and how many jobs wait. Click
  it to see the queue: whose jobs, what kind, when each starts. Your own have
  Cancel.
- Everyone in the family shares one card. Long videos are split into shots and
  mixed fairly with other people's short jobs.
- You get a phone notification when a job is ready; tapping it opens the
  Studio on that project.
- **⏸ Pause** (parents): *Pause after this job* lets the running job finish;
  *Pause now, requeue this job* stops it at once and puts it back in the queue
  to start over when you resume (half a video cannot be kept). ▶ Resume
  continues.
- The card is sometimes lent to the Programmer's local model or the coding
  benchmark; the bar says so, and jobs wait for it.

## Recording a tutorial (Recording or Free projects)

1. Press **🔴 Record**. Tick what to record: **Screen**, **Camera**,
   **Microphone**, **Computer sound**. Screen recording works in a computer's
   browser, not the phone app.
2. If you record the screen and the camera, choose where the camera starts out
   (a corner, or hidden) and its size. You can change it later.
3. **Start**. The browser asks what to share: pick a screen, a window or a tab.
   For the computer's sound, tick "Share audio" in that dialog (on Linux only a
   browser tab's sound can be shared; Windows and ChromeOS share the whole
   computer's sound).
4. **Pause** and **Stop** as needed. The recording uploads while you record, so
   closing the tab loses seconds, not the take.

The screen with your microphone, the camera and the computer's sound are
**three separate recordings**. Nothing is burnt into anything, so afterwards,
on the clip:

- **📷 Hidden ↖ ↗ ↙ ↘** and the size, in the row right under the player, choose
  whether and where the camera shows for the **whole clip**.
- **🎤 Microphone** and **🔊 Computer** sliders set each sound's volume (0–200%;
  above 100% is heard in the film, the browser plays at most 100%).
- Changes show at once while playing, and the film uses them.

### Cutting a recording into sections

Under a recording is a bar of its sections.

- **✂ Split here** splits at the playhead.
- Click a section to select it (the player jumps there). **✕ Cut out** removes
  it from playback and the film; **↺ Keep** puts it back.
- Each section can have **its own camera**: select the section, then pick in
  the **📷 menu next to ✕ Cut out**: *As the clip*, *Hidden*, or a corner. For
  example, show your face in the intro and hide it during the demo. (The row
  of 📷 buttons under the player is the whole clip's camera; a section's own
  choice wins over it.)
- **⇥ Join with next** merges a section with the following one; **Undo
  sections** goes back to the whole clip.
- Nothing is cut from the recording file, so everything can be undone.
- Sections belong to the version they were made on; on another version the
  clip plays whole.

### Cleaning up a recording

- **✂️ Take out silences** makes a new version without long pauses (more than
  1.2 s of quiet), leaving a little air so words are not clipped. It listens to
  the microphone only, and cuts the camera and computer sound at the same
  places.
- **🎚 Clean the microphone** makes a new version with background noise taken
  out and the loudness evened out to video-site level. Only the sound changes.
  Your sections move to the new version.
- **📝 Subtitles** transcribes what you said into subtitles (.srt, downloadable,
  and can be burnt into the film). With subtitles, **✨ Title and chapters**
  asks Alfred for a title, a description and chapters.

## A music video from a song

1. In **Music & voices**, make a song: write lyrics (or ask Alfred to write
   them with **Write the lyrics with Alfred**), choose a style, and generate it. Make a few
   versions and ⭐ the best.
2. To change a song: **✏️** — redo *a part* (tick or rewrite lines; only that
   stretch is sung again) or *all of it, close to this one*.
3. **🎬** on the song: the Studio listens to it (the beat, the sections, when
   each word is sung) and cuts the video on the music. **Plan the video**: Alfred
   writes a shot for each cut, using the project's look and its characters.
4. The **Storyboard** is drawn first: a picture per shot, about a minute each
   (a video shot takes about 25 minutes, so fix things here first).
5. Then the **Video** tab makes each shot from its approved picture.
6. **▶ Preview** plays the shots in order with the song; **Download with the
   music** saves that preview. **🎞️ Put the film together** renders the final
   video.

## The look and the characters

- The project's **look** ("3D cartoon, soft light, pastel colours") is the only
  place to say the style. Don't repeat the style in shot descriptions.
- **Characters**: a name, a look (how they appear, in words), a personality,
  pictures and a voice. Give a character a photo and the house's vision model
  writes their look from it (✍️ does it on request). A voice sample lets their
  lines be spoken in that voice (▶ to try).
- Characters start in their project; widen them (**↗ Use in all my projects**, or the
  family's) when you want to reuse them.
- Reference pictures: in **Files**, 🎨 marks a picture as the film's style
  (two at most). A shot can have its own reference picture (its setting or
  composition). Frames with pictures to go on are drawn from them.

## The storyboard

- Each frame is reviewed automatically for what the shot should show and for
  the look. Under 7/10, Alfred suggests a better description: **Use and
  redraw**.
- **🔁 Refine** runs that review-and-redraw loop on every frame (twice at most).
- **One correction for every shot**: the box under the toolbar ("it is night in
  every shot") changes every description the same way and redraws them. A
  correction about the whole style changes the look instead.
- **📌** makes any picture in the project a shot's frame. ✕ removes a shot.
- **Redraw the changed ones** redraws frames whose description changed.

## Sheet music and practice

**🎼 Sheet music** on a song version writes out its parts (guitar with tablature and
chords, piano), about a minute for a 90-second song, and opens the practice
page: play along with the song, the song without the part, the part alone or
a synthesizer, at 25–125% speed, with loops. It is written by ear: chords and
rhythm are usually right, fast runs may not be.

## Alfred and the Studio

In the chat you can ask Alfred to make a picture, a song (he writes the
lyrics), an instrumental or a short video, to tell you the queue, or to work on
a project by name: its characters, its storyboard, its music video. He says
where the job is in the queue and you are notified when it is ready.

## Good to know

- Video is slow: about 5 minutes of card time per second of video. A full music
  video is an overnight queue.
- Films are saved as H.265: phones, the app and Safari play them; Firefox and
  most Linux desktop browsers do not.
- Faces can still change between video shots; the storyboard keeps them
  closer.
- In the clip player, fullscreen shows the screen without the camera overlay;
  the film has it.
